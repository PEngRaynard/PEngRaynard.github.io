Making flood risk to bridges measurable where data is scarce

I am a civil engineer and PhD researcher at University College London. I have designed bridges and drainage in Tanzania, and I now use satellite imagery to work out which river crossings are most at risk.

My work starts from a practical problem: engineers and road agencies have to decide where to spend limited money without the river gauges and damage records that richer countries rely on.

Research

Flood vulnerability from space

Developing satellite-based ways to judge how flooding affects bridges and culverts in regions with few gauges and little ground survey data.

Fragility and consequences

Linking how likely a structure is to be damaged by a flood to what that damage means for the people and economy that depend on the road.

Resilience of road networks in Tanzania

Looking at river crossings as a network, so that limited budgets go first to the structures whose failure would cut communities off.

Earth observation for agriculture

Exploring how satellite imagery can give smallholder farmers better information about the land they work.

This is ongoing PhD work. Details will appear here as papers are published.
